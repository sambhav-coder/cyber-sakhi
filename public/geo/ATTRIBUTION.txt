DataMeet India state boundaries (simplified display derivative).
Source: https://github.com/datameet/maps
  (website/docs/data/geojson/states.geojson, "India - State Boundaries")

Local processing (documented, no geometry invented):
- Kept the single ST_NM name property per feature; dropped nothing else.
- Douglas-Peucker simplification (tolerance ~0.02 degrees) + rounding to 3
  decimals shrank 15.7 MB to ~138 KB for browser use. No vertex was added
  or moved beyond the tolerance; small-UT polygons were verified present
  (36/36 features intact).
- Served as a static runtime asset (public/geo/india-states.geojson), never
  bundled into JavaScript.

Known dataset limitations (from DataMeet's own notes, inherited here):
- Names follow the dataset verbatim, including "Arunanchal Pradesh" and
  "Dadara & Nagar Havelli" spellings; the display join table maps to these
  exact strings.
- Jammu & Kashmir is pre-bifurcation geometry (Ladakh has no separate
  polygon); DNH and Daman & Diu remain separate features (pre-merger).
- Boundaries are community-digitized and approximate — suitable for a
  case-volume choropleth, not for legal or official use.

Surrounding regional context (neighboring landmasses only; India remains
the sole extruded data layer):
- Source: world-atlas countries-110m (already a project dependency),
  whose geometries derive from Natural Earth (public domain). Rendered
  flat, dark, and non-interactive; a country appears iff its real bounding
  box intersects the regional view — no hand-picked list, no redrawn
  borders. Sea labels (Arabian Sea, Bay of Bengal, Indian Ocean) are
  cartographic text at mid-region positions only; oceans carry no boundary
  lines of any kind.
- world-atlas 110m is coarse by design: adequate for subdued context, not
  for precise borders. No legal or official accuracy is claimed for any
  geometry in this scene.
